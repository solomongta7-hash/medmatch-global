What a Straumann Implant Costs in Türkiye, Canada and the U.S.

Short answer

A Straumann BLX fixture costs around $890 at our partner clinics in Türkiye, against roughly $5,000 in the United States and $4,300 in Canada. The hardware is identical and imported from the same Swiss factory — the gap is clinic overhead, labour and rent, not a cheaper implant.

Prices on this page are MedMatch Global's own published figures, checked against our partner clinics' current written price lists on August 11, 2026. Comparison figures for the U.S. and Canada are published private-pay averages, sourced separately. This page is general information, not medical advice.

Why the same implant costs a sixth as much

The implant is a machined titanium screw with a proprietary surface treatment. Straumann sells it to a clinic in Antalya at a comparable wholesale price to a clinic in Ontario — that part of the cost barely moves. What moves is everything around it: surgical staff salaries, clinic rent, lab technician wages, sterilisation staffing, professional liability cover and marketing spend, all of which are dramatically cheaper in Türkiye. Add a currency rate that has favoured the dollar for years and you get a sixfold difference in the invoice with no difference in the box the implant came out of.

That is also why the savings on hardware-heavy treatment are smaller than the savings on labour-heavy treatment. A Straumann case saves less proportionally than an Osstem case, because a bigger share of the Straumann price is the part that does not get cheaper.

Is a cheap Turkish implant really Straumann?

Sometimes it is not, and this is the single most useful thing to check. Some clinics quote a headline price built on a budget system and only name the brand if you ask. Others quote "premium European implants" without naming anything at all. Neither is necessarily fraud, but neither is a quote you can compare.

Ask for three things in writing before you pay anything:

  1. The brand and product line by name — "Straumann BLX", not "Swiss implant".
  2. The implant passport or certificate, handed to you after surgery. Every major manufacturer supplies one, with the lot number and reference code. It is what your dentist at home will need.
  3. Confirmation that the quoted brand is what will be placed, with the price for an alternative if the surgeon changes plan mid-procedure.
If a clinic will not name the implant system in writing, that is not a price question — it is a disqualifying answer. Every MedMatch quote names the system before you book.

Is Osstem, at $450, a bad implant?

No. Osstem is the largest implant manufacturer in Asia, has a long clinical record and a well-documented surface, and is widely used across Europe and Korea. Neodent, at a similar price, is part of the Straumann group itself. The premium systems buy you more published long-term data, a wider prosthetic component range, and the near-certainty that any dentist anywhere can service the implant in fifteen years.

The honest framing: brand matters most for serviceability decades from now and least for whether the implant integrates this year. If you are 45 and expect to need work on it in 2050, pay for the documented system. If budget is genuinely the constraint, a well-placed Osstem from a good surgeon beats a badly placed Straumann every time. Placement quality outranks brand.

Will my dentist at home work on it?

For the major systems, yes — Straumann, Nobel Biocare, BEGO, Osstem and Neodent components are all available through normal North American dental supply channels. The thing that makes servicing hard is not the country the implant was placed in, it is an obscure system with no local parts. That is the real argument for a documented brand.

Take home your implant passport, the CBCT scan, and a written note of the abutment type. With those three, a dentist in Toronto or Ohio can order the right parts without contacting the clinic in Türkiye at all.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Straumann implant cost in Turkey compared to Canada?+

Around $890 for the fixture in Türkiye against roughly $4,300 in Canada and $5,000 in the United States. The implant is manufactured in Switzerland and imported in all three cases — the price difference is clinic overhead, staffing and rent.

Which implant brands do Turkish clinics use?+

The commonly placed systems are Osstem and Neodent at the value end (around $450), BEGO and Hiossen in the middle, and Nobel Biocare ($590) and Straumann BLX ($890) at the premium end. All are imported from the same manufacturers used in North America.

How do I know I actually got the implant I paid for?+

Ask for the implant passport or certificate before you leave the clinic. Every major manufacturer issues one carrying the lot number and reference code. If a clinic cannot produce it, you have no way to verify what was placed — and neither will your dentist at home.

Is Osstem a good implant brand?+

Yes. It is the largest manufacturer in Asia with a long clinical record and a well-documented surface. Premium systems buy more published long-term data and wider parts availability decades out. For whether the implant integrates now, surgical placement quality matters more than the brand on the box.

Can my dentist in Canada or the U.S. service an implant placed in Turkey?+

For any of the major systems, yes — components are available through normal North American supply channels. Take home the implant passport, your CBCT scan and a written note of the abutment type, and your dentist can order parts without ever contacting the Turkish clinic.

Does a premium implant justify the extra cost?+

It buys long-term documentation and parts availability, which matters most if you are young enough to need work on it in twenty or thirty years. It does not buy a better surgeon. Given a fixed budget, put it into placement quality first and brand second.
