Dental Implants in Turkey: What a Canadian Patient Actually Pays

Short answer

A single dental implant with its crown costs about CA$1,466 all-in in Türkiye through MedMatch Global — treatment, hotel for both visits, transfers and our flat fee — against roughly CA$4,000 to CA$8,000 in Canada. A full arch (All-on-4) is about CA$7,576 versus CA$25,000–CA$35,000 at home. Flights are extra, and implants need two trips.

What the same work costs in Canada

Canadian provincial fee guides put implant placement in the CA$1,900–CA$2,500 band, the abutment around CA$700, and the crown between CA$1,600 and CA$2,200. Specialists routinely charge above guide. That lands a single restored implant somewhere between CA$4,000 and CA$8,000, and the wider spread is mostly which implant system your surgeon uses and whether you need a graft.

Full-arch work is where the gap stops being a discount and starts being a different order of magnitude. All-on-4 for one arch is commonly quoted at CA$25,000 to CA$35,000 in Canada; both arches routinely passes CA$50,000. Provincial health plans do not cover any of it, and most private plans cap dental benefits at CA$1,500–CA$2,500 a year, which does not move the number meaningfully.

Add the flights — twice

Implants are not a one-trip treatment. The fixture has to fuse with the jawbone before it can carry a crown, which takes eight to ten weeks and happens at home, not in a hotel. So budget two return flights, not one.

From Toronto or Montreal, a return flight to Istanbul or Antalya typically runs CA$1,070 to CA$1,650; from Vancouver or Calgary, CA$1,340 to CA$1,990. Doubled, that is roughly CA$2,100–CA$4,000 on top of the treatment.

The honest arithmetic for one implant: CA$1,466 of treatment plus about CA$2,100 of flights is roughly CA$3,600 — against CA$4,000–CA$8,000 at home. Real, but thin. One implant is not usually worth the flights. Three or more, or a full arch, is where the gap stops being marginal.

Will my Canadian plan cover any of it?

Usually not, and you should assume not. Provincial health plans do not fund implants at home or abroad. Some private group plans will reimburse a portion of out-of-country dental work at their own fee-guide rate, but the majority of Canadian patients who travel are paying out of pocket by choice — the annual cap has already been exhausted or the treatment was never eligible.

Two things are worth doing before you travel, and both are free: ask your plan administrator in writing whether out-of-country dental claims are eligible, and ask us for an itemised invoice with procedure codes. If your plan does reimburse, it will want the codes.

What our price does not include

Said plainly, because this is where cheap quotes go wrong. Our all-in figure covers treatment, your hotel nights, airport and clinic transfers, a personal host, and coordination. It does not cover:

  • International flights, on either trip.
  • Bone grafting or a sinus lift, if your scan shows you need one — these are quoted after your x-rays are reviewed, not guessed at beforehand.
  • Food and anything you spend outside the clinic and hotel.
  • A companion's flight or their share of the room, if you bring someone.

If your scan does show a graft, you will get the revised number in writing before you book anything — not on arrival. A quote that changes at the clinic door is the single most common complaint in this industry, and it is entirely avoidable.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dental implant cost in Turkey for a Canadian patient?+

About CA$1,466 all-in for a single implant with its crown, which covers the treatment, hotel nights for both visits, VIP transfers and the flat coordination fee. Flights are extra and you will need two of them. In Canada the same restored implant typically costs CA$4,000 to CA$8,000.

Is it worth flying from Toronto to Turkey for one implant?+

Honestly, usually not. Add roughly CA$2,100 of flights across two trips and a single implant saves a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars. From about three implants up, or any full-arch case, the gap becomes large enough to carry the travel comfortably.

Will my Canadian dental plan cover implants done in Turkey?+

Provincial health plans do not cover implants anywhere. Some private group benefits plans reimburse out-of-country dental work at their own fee-guide rate, but most Canadian patients who travel are paying out of pocket. Ask your plan administrator in writing first, and ask us for an itemised invoice with procedure codes.

How many trips to Turkey do dental implants need?+

Two, about eight to ten weeks apart. The first trip places the implants, the second fits the final crowns. Each trip is roughly four days on the ground. The healing in between happens at home.

Are the implants used in Turkey the same brands as in Canada?+

At a properly vetted clinic, yes — Straumann, Nobel Biocare, BEGO and Osstem are all placed in Türkiye and all are serviceable by a Canadian dentist. Ask for the brand in writing before you pay. A quote that will not name the system is a quote to walk away from.

What if I need a bone graft?+

It is quoted separately once your CBCT scan has been reviewed, typically around $350 per site for a graft and $700 for a sinus lift, against roughly $700 and $1,900 in Canada. You get the revised total in writing before booking, never on arrival.

Do I pay MedMatch or the clinic?+

The treatment cost is paid directly to the clinic — MedMatch takes no percentage of it and adds no markup. What you pay us is a single flat $300 Patient Care & Coordination Fee, the same amount whether your treatment costs $550 or $8,000.
